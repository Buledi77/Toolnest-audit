About ToolNest: research, evidence and editorial policy

How ToolNest researches software and AI tools, what our evidence labels mean, and how we handle pricing, affiliate links and corrections.

ToolNest helps people choose software and AI tools. You start from a decision: compare two products, find a tool for a use case, or check what to try instead. Our pages explain the trade-offs in plain English and name the buyer each product does not suit. They are written by Abdul Muswir, Founder of ToolNest.

Who runs ToolNest

ToolNest was founded by Abdul Muswir, who researches and writes the pages on this site. If you spot something wrong or out of date, tell us and we will check it against the official source.

How we research products

Most of what we publish is built from information the vendor publishes itself:

  • Official product and feature pages
  • Official pricing pages, including plan limits and billing terms
  • Official documentation, help centres and changelogs
  • Published licence, refund and cancellation terms

We use those sources to answer the questions a buyer actually asks: what the product does, what it costs, what the free tier allows, where it stops being a good fit, and what switching involves. We do not present vendor marketing as independent testing.

Source-verified versus hands-on tested

These are different claims, and we label them differently.

Source-verified means a commercial or product fact was read on an official vendor page on the date shown. It describes what the vendor publishes, not what we observed in use.

Hands-on tested means we used the product ourselves and can describe the experience. We only say this on a page when it is true, and we do not publish test hours, scores or screenshots we cannot stand behind.

Pages that quote prices or plan limits carry the label “Source-verified; not hands-on tested” unless the page explicitly says otherwise. We have not personally tested every product on this site, and we do not claim to have done so.

How we check pricing and commercial facts

  • We read the vendor's own pricing page in the currency the vendor publishes, and we say which date we checked it.
  • We distinguish promotional prices from list prices and renewal rates. Where a promotion requires a long term, we say so.
  • We do not convert currencies or invent local pricing. Taxes, regional offers and currency conversion are left to the vendor's checkout.
  • Where a plan is priced by usage, contacts or subscribers, we describe the pricing structure instead of quoting a single figure that may not apply to you.
  • If an official page cannot be read, we say so on the page and avoid quoting numbers we could not verify.

Prices, limits and packaging change without notice. Treat every figure as a dated snapshot and confirm it on the official site before you buy.

How we choose tools

  • One job, done well: each tool has a clear main use.
  • Easy to start: a beginner can reach a first useful result without a long setup.
  • Clear about what it is for: we can say who it suits and who should skip it.
  • Honest about trade-offs: every review lists drawbacks and the buyer who should choose something else.
  • Verifiable facts: where a claim matters to a purchase, we link the official source.

How the pages are organised

Each tool has its own research profile. We also publish side-by-side comparisons, alternatives pages, use-case guides and shortlists so you can narrow a decision before opening a vendor site.

Editorial independence

Our editorial choices are ours alone. Nobody can pay to be listed, ranked higher or reviewed more kindly. A commission rate is never a reason to include a product, place it first or soften a criticism. If we ever receive a sponsored placement or a free account, we will say so on the page where it applies.

Affiliate links and disclosure

Some links on ToolNest are affiliate links. If you buy through one, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. That commission does not guarantee placement, a positive recommendation or a better position in a comparison. Where a product has no affiliate programme, we still link to its official site when it is the right answer.

The full list of link types is explained in our affiliate disclosure.

How updates and corrections work

We update a page when a material fact changes: a price, a plan limit, a free-tier allowance, a product being discontinued or a vendor changing its terms. The date shown on a page refers to the last check of its commercial facts, not a claim that the whole page was re-tested.

We do not add a new date to make a page look fresh. If nothing material has changed, the existing date stays.

To report a correction, contact us with the page URL, the sentence you believe is wrong and, if possible, the official source that shows the current position. We will check it and correct the page, or explain why we are keeping the wording.

What we do not do

  • We do not claim to have tested every product.
  • We do not invent prices, discounts, performance results, ratings, testimonials or user numbers.
  • We do not accept payment for a positive verdict.
  • We do not publish a review date without a corresponding check of the page's commercial facts.
