Head-to-head comparison

IFTTT vs Make

Make is the better automation builder once a workflow branches. IFTTT remains cheaper and faster for a single trigger and action. The choice is between a consumer remote control and a visual workflow canvas.

Jump to pricingJump to verdict

The short answer

Our verdict

IFTTT is cheaper and simpler; Make is more capable and still affordable. If your automation fits in one sentence, IFTTT is the right tool. If you need to describe it with 'if', 'unless' and 'then also', Make's visual canvas will save you from building brittle chains of Applets.

Choose IFTTT if…

The job is a single trigger with one or two actions, and you do not need to branch, merge or transform data between apps.

  • You want a simple if-this-then-that Applet
  • Consumer apps, social accounts or smart devices are the target
  • You value a five-minute setup over deep configuration
  • The lowest monthly cost is a priority
Visit IFTTT (opens the official site in a new tab)

Choose Make if…

The job involves multiple apps, conditional routes, iterators or error handling, and you want the logic visible in one place.

  • Workflows need routers, filters or data transformation
  • You connect several business apps in one scenario
  • You want to see and debug the flow visually
  • You can spend an afternoon learning the builder

Which workflow fits you

IFTTT's credit model is simple: an Applet runs, credits are consumed. That makes it easy to forecast and easy to abandon if a recipe is not useful. Make's credit model is more granular: every module action in a scenario consumes a credit, so a ten-module scenario uses far more than a single trigger-action Applet. The headline 10,000 credits on Make's Core plan can disappear quickly if your scenarios are large and run often.

What you buy with those credits is control. Make lets you branch with routers, iterate over arrays, format dates and text, handle errors and inspect each run module by module. That is the difference between automating a notification and automating a process. IFTTT's queries and filters are genuinely useful, but they are refinements on a trigger-action model rather than a full workflow engine.

The free tiers tell the same story. IFTTT lets you build unlimited Applets and gives you 1,000 credits a month, which is enough to run a handful of personal automations indefinitely. Make gives you 1,000 credits but only two active scenarios, so it is a trial of the canvas rather than a free automation service.

Choose based on the number of decisions in the workflow. Zero or one decision: IFTTT. Two or more, with different actions per outcome: Make. If you are unsure, sketch the flow on paper first; the number of branches will make the answer obvious.

Frequently asked questions

Is Make better than IFTTT?

For multi-step workflows with branching, yes. For a single trigger and action, IFTTT is simpler and cheaper. 'Better' depends on the complexity of the job.

Can Make replace IFTTT for free?

Not really. Make's free plan allows only two active scenarios, while IFTTT allows unlimited Applets. Make's free tier is for evaluating the builder.

Which has more integrations?

Make advertises 3,500+ apps and IFTTT 1,000+ services. Make skews toward business software; IFTTT skews toward consumer apps and devices.

How do credits differ?

IFTTT charges credits per Applet run. Make charges credits per module action, so a multi-step scenario can consume several credits per run.
