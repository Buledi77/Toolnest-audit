Ahrefs vs Moz Pro

Ahrefs is built around its own web index: backlinks, keywords and competitor research. Moz Pro is a smaller, calmer SEO workspace with strong teaching material. Choose by the data you need and how much interface you will actually use.

The short answer

Our verdict

Ahrefs is the stronger choice when backlinks and keyword research are the core of the job, especially for agencies and link-led SEO. Moz Pro is the better fit for a single site owner who wants rank tracking, crawling and keyword research in a simpler, lower-priced workspace.

Ahrefs has the lowest headline entry price through its Starter tier, but that is a limited entry plan; check its monthly usage limits before treating it as a full replacement. Moz Pro's first full campaign plan is $99 per month, or $79 billed yearly, with a free trial.

Choose Ahrefs if…

Link data and keyword research are the product, and you want one index to interrogate.

  • Backlink analysis and link-gap work are central to your job
  • You want a large keyword database with competitor research beside it
  • You need rank tracking and site audits in the same account
  • You will use the data weekly, not once a quarter

Choose Moz Pro if…

You want one site monitored, explained and improved without learning a research suite.

  • Your work is mostly your own site's rankings, crawl health and keywords
  • You want a lower-priced full workspace and a free trial
  • You value built-in teaching material and a simpler interface
  • You do not need paid-search or deep competitor research

Who should skip each product — and who should choose neither

Skip Ahrefs if…

  • The plan you need costs more than your SEO work returns
  • You only need a technical crawl and on-page checks
  • You want the simplest possible interface with guided teaching
  • You need a permanent free plan for ongoing work

Skip Moz Pro if…

  • You need the largest available backlink index
  • You bill competitor and content research
  • You manage many client sites with complex reporting
  • You need advanced automation or API access

Choose neither when

Choose neither if you only need a technical crawl: Screaming Frog's free tier covers up to 500 URLs and its paid licence is a flat annual cost. If you need paid-search research as well, Semrush is the more direct comparison. If your site is small and your needs are occasional, free tools and a consultant may be cheaper than either subscription.

How to decide: buyer criteria that matter

Ignore the feature list on the pricing page until you have answered these questions. They decide which plan you actually need.

Backlinks or on-page first

If link data decides your strategy, weight the index. If fixing your own pages decides it, weight the audit and workflow.

Data you can verify

Check sample domains and keywords from your market in a trial or free account before committing.

Plan allowance

Compare credits, projects and export rows, not just the headline monthly price.

Team fit

A data-heavy interface saves time for a specialist and slows down a generalist.

Reporting

Client-facing reports and scheduled exports matter more than another chart.

Commitment

Annual plans reduce the monthly rate but lock in the spend. Check renewal and cancellation terms.

Genuine limitations

Every tool is a compromise. These are the drawbacks we would want to know before paying.

Ahrefs limitations

  • No free trial; the free account is limited
  • The plan you need may cost more than Moz Pro's entry tier
  • The interface assumes familiarity with SEO data
  • Plan credits and usage limits still constrain heavy research

Moz Pro limitations

  • The backlink index is smaller than Ahrefs'
  • Fewer research datasets and integrations
  • Less suitable for high-volume link-gap work
  • Campaign and keyword caps apply by tier

Switching, export and migration

There is no direct account-to-account migration. Your export files are the only portable asset, and historical charts stay with the vendor you leave. Plan the switch at the end of a reporting period, not mid-campaign.

Moving from Moz Pro to Ahrefs

  • Export projects, keyword lists and backlink reports as CSV before cancelling.
  • Rebuild tracked keywords and projects in Ahrefs; history does not import.
  • Expect backlink counts and keyword volumes to differ between indexes.
  • Keep both accounts for one reporting cycle while you validate the new data.

Moving from Ahrefs to Moz Pro

  • Export keyword lists, backlink reports and crawl issues before cancelling.
  • Recreate campaigns in Moz Pro; ranking history does not transfer.
  • Recheck which integrations and exports your workflow depends on.
  • Cancel before the renewal date and keep a copy of final reports.
Plan for overlap. If you are switching because of price, confirm the new plan's limits first. Moving to a cheaper tier that cannot hold your keyword list simply moves the cost into manual work.

Use-case conclusion

Link-led SEO or digital PR

Ahrefs — the backlink index and prospecting workflow are the reason to pay.

Single site, modest budget

Moz Pro — Standard covers the recurring work with a trial and a lower full-plan price.

Large keyword map

Ahrefs — built for broad research and competitor keyword coverage.

Client-friendly monthly report

Moz Pro — simpler dashboards are easier to explain.

Technical crawl only

Neither — compare Screaming Frog for a flat annual licence.

Paid and organic research together

Neither — Semrush is the more direct fit.

Alternatives worth considering

Both products are strong at different jobs. If neither fits, the right alternative is usually determined by whether you need a crawler, a link index, or a broader marketing suite.

Frequently asked questions

Is Ahrefs better than Moz Pro?

For backlink analysis and broad keyword research, Ahrefs is usually the stronger product. For a single site owner who wants a simpler workspace, Moz Pro is often enough and easier to learn. 'Better' depends on which dataset your work depends on.

Does Ahrefs have a free trial?

No free trial is advertised. Ahrefs offers a limited free account and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools. Moz Pro offers a free trial, which makes it easier to evaluate the full workflow before paying.

Which is cheaper?

Ahrefs has the lowest headline entry price at $29 per month for its Starter plan, but that plan has a smaller allowance than the larger tiers. Moz Pro Standard is $99 per month, or $79 billed yearly. Compare the plan that covers the whole job.

Can I move my projects between them?

Not automatically. Export keyword lists, crawl issues and reports, then rebuild projects in the new tool. Historical rankings and backlink counts will differ because each vendor uses a different index.

Which is better for a beginner?

Moz Pro is easier to start with because the campaign structure and teaching material are more guided. Ahrefs rewards users who already know which report they need.
