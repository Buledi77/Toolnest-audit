Head-to-head comparison

Make vs Zapier

Make is the better value for teams that will invest time in the canvas. Zapier is the safer choice for non-technical teams that need broad app coverage and support. The price difference is large; so is the difference in polish.

The short answer

Our verdict

Make usually wins on cost and workflow control. Zapier usually wins on ease, app coverage and organisational trust. A small team with one person who enjoys automation should try Make first; a company rolling automation out to non-technical departments should pay for Zapier.

Choose Make if…

You will build and maintain the workflows yourself, and you want branching, data transformation and lower per-operation costs.

  • You want more workflow power per dollar
  • You will learn a visual scenario builder
  • You need routers, iterators and error handlers
  • You can tolerate a denser interface

Choose Zapier if…

Automation is used by several people, app coverage is uncertain, and you value support and a gentler learning curve over the lowest price.

  • You need the widest possible app directory
  • Non-technical staff will build and maintain workflows

Which workflow fits you

Make's pricing advantage is real but easy to overstate. At $9 per month for 10,000 credits, it looks an order of magnitude cheaper than Zapier. Yet a credit is consumed per module action, and a serious scenario can use five to fifteen credits per run, while Zapier's task count also rises with each step. The correct comparison is cost per completed workflow at your actual volume, not cost per thousand credits.

The interface is the second dividing line. Make shows the whole scenario as a canvas: modules, routes, filters and error paths. That is empowering once you understand it, and confusing on day one. Zapier's linear editor hides complexity, which makes simple Zaps approachable but complex ones harder to visualise. Neither is objectively better; they optimise for different users.

App coverage is where Zapier earns its premium. With 9,000+ integrations, the chance that an obscure SaaS tool already has a connector is much higher. Make's 3,500+ apps cover the common business stack, and its HTTP module can reach anything with an API, but that requires more setup and technical confidence.

Think about who will own the automation after launch. If it is one operations person who enjoys the tool, Make's lower cost and deeper control are attractive. If it is a rotating group of marketers with no engineering support, Zapier's guardrails, templates and support reduce the risk of abandoned workflows.

Frequently asked questions

Is Make cheaper than Zapier?

Yes at comparable volume. Make Core is $9 per month billed annually for 10,000 credits; Zapier Professional starts at $19.99 per month billed annually for 750 tasks. Compare cost per completed workflow, not the raw unit counts.

Does Zapier have more integrations?

Yes. Zapier advertises 9,000+ apps; Make advertises 3,500+. Make can still reach other services through HTTP requests, but that requires more setup.

Which is easier to learn?

Zapier. Its linear editor and templates are designed for non-technical users. Make's canvas is more powerful but assumes you will learn its model.

Can Make handle complex workflows?

Yes. Routers, iterators, aggregators and error handlers make it suitable for multi-branch business processes that would be awkward in Zapier.
