Head-to-head comparison

n8n vs Zapier

n8n is for teams that want to own their automation layer. Zapier is for teams that want it to work without owning anything. The free self-hosted option is genuinely powerful, but it shifts operational risk onto you.

The short answer

Our verdict

Zapier is the lower-risk default for most businesses. n8n is the better long-term platform for technical teams with high workflow volume or strict data requirements. The free Community Edition is not really free once you count engineering time, but at scale it can be the cheapest option by a wide margin.

Choose n8n if…

You have engineering capacity, complex workflows, or data that cannot leave your infrastructure, and you want to stop paying per step.

  • You can self-host or will pay for n8n Cloud
  • Workflows need code, HTTP calls or unlimited steps
  • You want execution-based pricing and data control
  • You have technical staff to maintain it

Choose Zapier if…

You want automation to be someone else's operational problem, with the broadest app library and a familiar interface.

  • You want a managed service with no infrastructure
  • App coverage across business SaaS is the priority
  • Non-technical staff will build workflows
  • You need team governance and mature support

Which workflow fits you

The economic argument for n8n is strongest when workflows are long. Zapier counts a task for every successful step, so a 20-step process can consume 20 tasks per run. n8n counts one execution for the whole run. At high volume and high step count, the difference compounds quickly, which is why technical teams often move to n8n as their automation estate grows.

The economic argument for Zapier is strongest when people are the scarce resource. A managed service has no server to patch, no database to back up and no upgrade that breaks a workflow at 2am. For a marketing team without engineering support, that operational simplicity is worth more than the per-task savings.

App coverage reinforces the same split. Zapier's 9,000+ integrations mean most business tools are a dropdown away. n8n covers common tools natively and everything else through HTTP requests, which is more flexible but assumes you can read an API document. A team that cannot do that will spend the savings on setup time.

Data control is the third factor. Self-hosted n8n keeps credentials and execution data inside your infrastructure, which matters for regulated industries or internal systems that cannot be exposed to a third party. Zapier is a cloud service by design; if that is unacceptable, the decision is made for you.

A pragmatic path is to start with Zapier for revenue-critical, low-volume workflows, then move high-volume or code-heavy processes to n8n once the pattern is proven. That avoids a big migration before you know which automations actually matter.

Frequently asked questions

Is n8n cheaper than Zapier?

For long, high-volume workflows, usually yes, because n8n bills per execution rather than per step. Self-hosting is free under the Community Edition but carries infrastructure and maintenance costs. For a few simple Zaps, Zapier's free or entry tier can be cheaper overall.

Can n8n replace Zapier completely?

For a technical team, often. The main gaps are app coverage and operational convenience: some SaaS tools have no native n8n node, and self-hosting requires maintenance.

Is n8n hard to learn?

n8n Cloud is approachable for anyone familiar with APIs. Self-hosting adds server administration, upgrades and backups. Zapier is easier for non-technical users.

Which is better for sensitive data?

Self-hosted n8n, because execution data and credentials can remain on your own infrastructure. Zapier is a cloud service and processes data on its platform.
