beehiiv vs Mailchimp

beehiiv is built for publishing and growing a newsletter. Mailchimp is a broader marketing platform for campaigns, automations and customer email. The decision is about the business model behind your list, not the editor.

The short answer

Our verdict

beehiiv is the better fit when the newsletter is the business: publishing, referral growth and monetisation live in one workflow. Mailchimp is the better fit when email supports a shop, service business or broader marketing calendar and you need established automation and segmentation.

Do not choose on the free plan alone. Both free tiers are for evaluation; the paid tier that fits your list size and feature needs is the real price.

Choose beehiiv if…

The newsletter is the product, and you want publishing, growth and monetisation in one place.

  • You publish a newsletter as the main product, not a side campaign
  • You want referral and audience-growth tools built into the platform
  • You want monetisation options such as subscriptions, boosts or an ad network
  • You prefer a publishing workflow over a general campaign builder

Choose Mailchimp if…

Email supports a shop, service business or wider marketing calendar, and you need established automation and segmentation.

  • You run campaigns alongside a website, shop or service business
  • You need segmentation, journeys and A/B testing in one account
  • You want templates and integrations that a team already knows
  • You need contact and e-commerce data connected to email

Who should skip each product — and who should choose neither

Skip beehiiv if…

  • You need deep e-commerce or CRM integrations for a shop
  • You need complex multi-step automations across a sales pipeline
  • Your email is a small part of a wider marketing calendar
  • You need an established template and agency ecosystem

Skip Mailchimp if…

  • The newsletter itself is the product and monetisation is the goal
  • You want a simpler publishing-first workflow
  • You want referral growth and an ad network in the same platform
  • You need to keep costs predictable as the list grows

Choose neither when

Choose neither if you need transactional email from an application, a full sales CRM, or a blog with no email list. Those are different product categories. If you only send occasional announcements to a small list, a free tier may be enough, but check the send and contact caps first.

How to decide: buyer criteria that matter

Ignore the feature list on the pricing page until you have answered these questions. They decide which plan you actually need.

Business model

A publication monetises attention; a shop monetises transactions. Choose the platform built for your revenue.

List size and sends

Model the price at your current list and at double it. Both vendors scale pricing with usage.

Automation depth

List the journeys you actually run. Newsletter platforms are not CRM automation engines.

Monetisation

If ads, subscriptions or referrals are part of the plan, check which tier unlocks them.

Integrations

Confirm the shop, CRM, analytics and payment tools you depend on are supported.

Ownership and export

Check that you can export subscribers and content, and that your sending domain is yours.

Genuine limitations

Every tool is a compromise. These are the drawbacks we would want to know before paying.

beehiiv limitations

  • Paid tiers scale with subscriber count and feature level
  • Some growth and monetisation features are gated to paid plans
  • Free-plan limits on subscribers and sends apply
  • Fewer conventional campaign and CRM features than Mailchimp

Mailchimp limitations

  • Pricing scales with contact count and plan level
  • Free-plan contact and send caps restrict regular campaigns
  • The Standard promotion is time-limited, then pricing changes
  • Advanced automation and reporting are gated by tier

Switching, export and migration

Neither platform offers a complete one-click migration of automations, tags and historical reporting. Subscribers are portable; workflow logic is not. Plan the move for a quiet week and warn readers that a verification email may arrive.

Moving from Mailchimp to beehiiv

  • Export subscribers as CSV, including status and consent evidence.
  • Map tags and segments manually; group names rarely transfer cleanly.
  • Recreate automations and welcome sequences in the new platform.
  • Authenticate the sending domain with the required DNS records before the first send.

Moving from beehiiv to Mailchimp

  • Export subscribers and suppression lists from beehiiv before cancelling.
  • Rebuild signup forms, referral settings and monetisation features that Mailchimp does not replicate.
  • Re-verify the sending domain and test deliverability with a small segment.
  • Keep the old account active until the first campaign has sent cleanly.
Plan for overlap. Keep the old account for at least one full send cycle. If deliverability drops, you can compare sending-domain authentication and list hygiene before deleting anything.

Frequently asked questions

Is beehiiv cheaper than Mailchimp?

It depends on your list size and the features you need. beehiiv prices by subscriber tier; Mailchimp prices by contact tier and plan. Mailchimp's Standard plan was promoted at $20 per month for 12 months at the entry tier on 12 November 2026, but that promotion is time-limited. Compare the tier that covers your current list.

Which is better for a newsletter?

beehiiv is built around newsletter publishing, growth and monetisation, so it is usually the more direct fit. Mailchimp can send a newsletter, but its strengths are campaigns, segmentation and business integrations.

Can I migrate my subscribers?

Yes. Both platforms support subscriber export and import, usually as CSV. Tags, automations and historical reports do not transfer cleanly, so budget time to rebuild them and re-verify your sending domain.

Does Mailchimp still have a free plan?

Yes, with contact and monthly send caps. The limits are low for a growing list, so check the current allowance before importing an audience.

Which is better for e-commerce?

Mailchimp, because shop integrations, customer data and revenue reporting are part of its core workflow. beehiiv is designed for publications rather than product catalogues.
