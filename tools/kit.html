Kit review: creator email marketing without the enterprise weight

Kit, formerly ConvertKit, is an email platform built for creators rather than sales teams. It handles the list, the newsletter, the landing page and the digital product checkout without turning into a full CRM. Here is where that focus helps and where it stops.

Kit overview

Kit is a creator-focused email marketing platform. You collect subscribers with forms and landing pages, send broadcasts, tag and segment the list, and build visual automations for welcome sequences and launches. It also sells digital products and paid newsletter subscriptions from the same account.

Why it stands out

The free plan is unusually generous for a creator tool: up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited landing pages, forms and broadcasts. The paid plans add the automation builder, branding removal, SMS and integrations that most growing creators eventually need. That progression is the reason Kit is a common first paid email platform.

Who Kit is best for, and who should skip it

Best for

  • Newsletter writers and creators who want email, forms and a simple product checkout in one place
  • Course creators, coaches and podcasters building welcome sequences and launch funnels
  • Solo operators who want tagging and visual automations without an enterprise marketing suite
  • Small teams that need a shared list and a straightforward editor
  • Creators migrating from another platform who want free migration on a paid plan

Skip it if

  • You need a full sales CRM with deal pipelines and account management
  • You need heavy transactional email or a developer-first sending API
  • You need complex multi-channel journeys across email, SMS and WhatsApp
  • You are a large e-commerce brand that needs advanced product recommendations and segmentation
  • You need a large team on the free plan; it includes one user

Kit pros and cons

Pros

  • A free plan that supports up to 10,000 subscribers
  • Unlimited landing pages, forms and email broadcasts on every plan
  • Visual automations with split paths on Creator and above
  • Tagging and segmentation that are easy to understand
  • Digital products, paid newsletters and subscriptions built in
  • Free migration from another platform on a paid plan

Cons

  • The free plan excludes visual automations, branding removal, SMS and integrations
  • Paid pricing rises with subscriber count, so the headline price is only for the first tier
  • It is email-centric, not a full CRM or a broad multi-channel suite
  • Digital product sales carry a 3.5% + 30c transaction fee
  • Advanced team features, analytics and unlimited users require Pro
  • A very large list can make a specialist newsletter platform cheaper

Features that matter in practice

Visual automations

Build welcome sequences and launch flows with a visual builder, split paths and unlimited sequences on paid plans.

Landing pages and forms

Unlimited pages and opt-in forms, with templates and a custom domain on every plan.

Tagging and segmentation

Tag subscribers by source or behaviour and group tags into segments for more relevant sends.

Broadcasts and sequences

Send one-off newsletters or scheduled sequences; Kit advertises no sending limits on any plan.

Digital products

Sell digital products, paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions with a sales page and checkout.

Integrations and API

A published app directory, an API and a Kit MCP connection for AI tools on paid plans.

Free plan and trial

The free plan supports up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms and broadcasts. It does not include visual automations, branding removal, SMS or integrations; those start on Creator. The 14-day Creator/Pro trial does not require a credit card.

How it compares and what to try instead

Kit is the strongest fit when the list is the business and you want automations without a CRM. It is a weaker fit when email is one channel in a larger marketing stack.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kit free?

Yes. The free plan supports up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms and email broadcasts. Visual automations, branding removal, SMS and integrations require a paid plan.

How much does Kit cost?

Creator is $33/month, or $390 billed yearly, for 1,000 subscribers. Pro is $50/month in the first year ($593 billed first year) and renews at the standard $66/month rate. Prices rise with subscriber count.

Does Kit have automations?

Yes, on Creator and above. The visual automation builder supports sequences, split paths and unlimited automations. The free plan does not include them.

Can I migrate my list to Kit?

Kit offers free migration on paid plans. Export your subscribers and tags first, confirm custom fields and automations will map, and keep the old platform active until the new list is verified.

Is Kit good for selling digital products?

Yes. Kit includes sales pages, checkout and paid newsletter subscriptions. Digital product sales carry a 3.5% + 30c transaction fee, so compare the total against a dedicated checkout tool.
