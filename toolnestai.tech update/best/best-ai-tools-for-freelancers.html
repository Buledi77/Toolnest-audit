Best Tools for Freelancers

Freelancers sell their time, so the useful tools cut admin, keep client writing clear and help you find or win work. These picks cover each of those jobs.

How we picked

These picks come from the tools ToolNest currently covers. They are ordered by how many freelancers need the job each tool does, from everyday planning and writing to optional extras such as search research or your own site.

  • Saves real hours each week.
  • Easy to learn without a team to set it up.
  • Fair for one person to afford.
  • Grows as your client list grows.

Our picks

1

Notion

Productivity

Holds notes, tasks, client pages and a simple tracker in one workspace.

Best for
Solo freelancers who want one home for everything
Skip it if
You manage large teams with strict deadlines.
Why it ranks here
Every freelancer needs somewhere to keep notes, tasks and client pages, whatever service they sell.
Better option in this list
Grammarly, if your main work is writing for clients.
Try Notion (opens the official site in a new tab)Read the Notion review →
2

Grammarly

Writing

Keeps client emails and proposals clear and polite.

Best for
Anyone writing in English for clients
Skip it if
You want a tool to write drafts for you.
Why it ranks here
Clear client emails and proposals matter to almost every freelancer, and it needs little setup.
Better option in this list
Notion, if disorganised work is a bigger problem than unclear writing.
Try Grammarly (opens the official site in a new tab)Read the Grammarly review →
3

Jasper

Marketing

Speeds up ad copy and product page writing with templates and a saved brand voice.

Best for
Freelance marketers and copywriters
Skip it if
You only write marketing copy occasionally.
Why it ranks here
It is specialised: strong for marketing copy, less useful if you write rarely.
Better option in this list
Grammarly, if you edit your own writing rather than draft marketing copy.
Try Jasper (opens the official site in a new tab)Read the Jasper review →
4

Semrush

SEO

Shows what people search for and what competitors rank for, which helps you win and serve SEO clients.

Best for
Freelancers who offer content or SEO work
Skip it if
You do not work with websites or search traffic.
Why it ranks here
It pays off mainly for content and SEO work, so fewer freelancers need it.
Better option in this list
Notion or Grammarly, if your clients do not depend on search traffic.
Try Semrush (opens the official site in a new tab)Read the Semrush review →
5

Systeme.io

Sales

Lets you sell a service, course or email list from one account.

Best for
Freelancers adding a digital product
Skip it if
You need a large physical store.
Why it ranks here
It suits freelancers adding a digital product or email list, a step many take later.
Better option in this list
Hostinger, if you want a portfolio or client site rather than a sales funnel.
Try Systeme.io (opens the official site in a new tab)Read the Systeme.io review →
6

Hostinger

Hosting

Hosts a portfolio or client site on your own domain.

Best for
Freelancers who want their own website
Skip it if
You want a ready-made page with nothing to set up.
Why it ranks here
A website is one need among many, and not every freelancer needs their own yet.
Better option in this list
Systeme.io, if you only want to sell one service or product. Notion, if organisation comes first.
Try Hostinger (opens the official site in a new tab)Read the Hostinger review →
