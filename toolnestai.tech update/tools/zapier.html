Zapier review: the broadest integration library in automation

Zapier connects more business apps than any other no-code automation platform, and it is the easiest to hand to a non-technical team. You pay for that reach: task-based pricing makes multi-step workflows more expensive than per-execution alternatives.

Zapier overview

Zapier is a hosted automation platform built around Zaps: a trigger, one or more actions, and optional filters, paths, formatting and code steps. Its main asset is the app directory, which covers the long tail of business software far better than consumer-focused tools. AI features, Agents, Chatbots and an MCP server now sit alongside classic Zaps.

Why it stands out

When a workflow depends on a specific SaaS tool, Zapier is the most likely platform to support it without custom work. The editor is also the most approachable for people who have never built an automation before.

Who Zapier is best for, and who should skip it

Best for

  • Business workflows that connect CRM, support, finance or marketing tools
  • Multi-step Zaps with filters, paths and data formatting
  • Teams that need shared workspaces, 25 users and SSO
  • Non-technical staff who need a guided builder and support
  • Agencies building automations for clients on a familiar platform

Skip it if

  • You are cost-sensitive and your workflows are long; Make or n8n is cheaper at volume
  • You need self-hosting or data residency
  • Your workflows are simple trigger-action recipes; IFTTT is far cheaper
  • You need unlimited steps without task-based billing

Zapier pros and cons

Pros

  • The largest app directory in the category, with 9,000+ integrations
  • Multi-step Zaps, filters, paths, formatter and webhooks cover most business logic
  • Shared workspaces, folders, SSO and audit logs make it viable for teams
  • A 14-day Professional trial and a permanent free tier make evaluation easy
  • AI features are integrated into the builder rather than sold as a separate product
  • Support and documentation are mature compared with cheaper tools

Cons

  • Task-based pricing multiplies quickly: a five-step Zap can use up to five tasks per run
  • Entry price is high relative to Make or IFTTT for comparable volume
  • No self-hosted option; all execution happens on Zapier's platform
  • Premium apps and some features require paid plans
  • Live chat support is limited to higher task tiers
  • Complex Zaps can become hard to audit as they grow

Features that matter in practice

Multi-step Zaps

Trigger, actions, filters and paths in one workflow, with a visual editor.

App directory

9,000+ integrations, including the long tail of business SaaS and premium apps.

Webhooks and code

Connect anything with an API and run custom JavaScript or Python within runtime limits.

Tables and Forms

Lightweight data tables and forms that integrate with Zaps without consuming tasks for their own triggers.

Team governance

Shared workspaces, folder permissions, SSO and audit logs on the Team plan.

AI tooling

AI by Zapier, Copilot, Agents, Chatbots and MCP, drawing on the same task pool for core AI steps.

Free plan and trial

Yes. The Free plan includes 100 tasks per month, unlimited Zaps, two-step workflows and 15-minute polling. New accounts also get a 14-day trial of Professional without a credit card, which is the better way to test multi-step Zaps and premium apps.

How it compares and what to try instead

Zapier is the safe choice for business integrations and non-technical teams. The cost case weakens as workflows get longer, which is where Make and n8n enter the conversation.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zapier free?

Zapier has a permanent free plan with 100 tasks per month, unlimited Zaps and two-step workflows. It also offers a 14-day Professional trial. Most business use requires a paid plan.

How much does Zapier cost?

Professional starts at $19.99 per month billed annually for 750 tasks ($29.99 monthly). A 2,000-task Professional plan starts at $49 per month billed annually, and Team starts at $69 per month billed annually.

Why is Zapier more expensive than Make?

Zapier charges per task and invests heavily in app coverage, support and team features. Make charges per module action at a lower unit price but has a steeper learning curve and fewer integrations.

Is Zapier good for large workflows?

Yes for capability, but costs rise with each step. If you run long workflows at high volume, an execution-based tool such as n8n can be substantially cheaper.
