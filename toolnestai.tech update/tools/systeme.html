Systeme.io overview

Systeme.io is an all-in-one platform for selling online. It combines sales pages, an email list, checkout, online courses and automation, so you do not have to connect several separate tools.

Why we recommend it

It lets a beginner sell something online without learning five different tools, which is often what stops people launching.

Quick verdict

Our verdict

Systeme.io is a reasonable first stop if you want to sell a digital product, course or service from one account and would rather not connect a page builder, an email tool and a checkout yourself.

It is a weaker fit if you need a fully custom site design, a large physical store or a long list of third-party integrations.

It has a free plan, so you can try a small project before paying. Limits change, so check them on the official site first.

This review is based on Systeme.io's official product, feature, help and pricing pages. It does not include hands-on test results.

Who Systeme.io is best for

It tends to suit people who want one account to cover the basics of selling online.

  • Beginners launching a first product, course or email list
  • Creators who want one tool instead of five
  • Small businesses selling digital products
  • Coaches and freelancers who need a simple page, a follow-up email and a way to take payments
  • People who want to run a small affiliate programme for their own offer

A quick test: if you can describe your offer in one sentence and the steps a buyer takes in a few clicks, an all-in-one tool is usually easier to manage.

Who should skip Systeme.io

  • Large stores that need advanced inventory and shipping tools
  • Designers who want pixel-perfect custom sites
  • Teams already happy with a stack of specialist tools
  • Anyone who relies on a specific third-party integration that Systeme.io does not list

Skipping it does not make it a bad tool. It means something else may fit better. For a full website, see Hostinger. To compare approaches, read Hostinger vs Systeme.io.

What a beginner can build with it

Systeme.io's official pages list these building blocks. Start with one, not all of them.

  • A landing page that collects email addresses and sends an automatic welcome email
  • A simple sales page with a checkout for one digital product
  • A small online course with lessons people pay to access
  • A basic blog or website
  • A booking page for calls or appointments
  • A small affiliate programme for your own product

The official feature list also mentions physical products, text-message campaigns, communities and automated webinars. Treat those as later steps once the basics work.

Main features explained in plain English

Sales funnels and pages

A funnel is a short path a visitor follows: a page, an offer, then checkout. You start from a template and edit it.

Email marketing

Collect subscribers with a form, then send one-off newsletters or a timed series of emails.

Online courses

Add lessons and sell access to them.

Checkout and payments

Take payment for products and courses. Help pages cover PayPal, Stripe and Razorpay, so check which one works where you live.

Automation

Simple rules such as "when someone buys, tag them and send an email". How many rules you get can depend on your plan.

Website and blog

Build a basic site or blog and connect your own domain.

Affiliate tools

Let other people promote your product and keep track of what they refer.

Booking calendar

Let people book time with you.

Important limitations and trade-offs

Plan limits can cap growth. The pricing page ties limits such as contacts, funnels, courses and automation rules to your plan, so a growing business may need to upgrade.

Design is simpler than dedicated page builders. Templates save time, but you get less fine control.

Integrations are narrower than on larger platforms. Official pages mention a public API and help pages cover several payment gateways. Check that every tool you rely on connects.

Email still needs setup. Help pages describe authenticating your sending domain. Skipping that step can affect whether your emails reach inboxes.

Moving later takes work. Pages, courses and email history may not transfer neatly, so check what you can export before building a lot.

Not built for large physical stores. Shipping and inventory needs are better served by a dedicated store platform.

Free plan: what to verify on the official site

Official Systeme.io pages describe a free plan that does not need a credit card. Limits on free plans change, and different official pages can show different numbers. Read the official pricing page (opens in a new tab) and its FAQ before you rely on it.

Check these before you start

  • How many contacts you can store
  • How many funnels, courses and blogs you can create
  • Whether you can connect your own domain
  • How many automation rules and email campaigns are included
  • Whether payments and affiliate tools are included
  • Whether a student or member cap applies
  • What happens when you reach a limit

Use the free plan to build one small project from start to finish. That tells you more than reading a feature list.

Paid plans: what to verify before upgrading

Plan names, prices and limits change, so we do not list prices here. Confirm everything on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Before you upgrade

  • What each plan allows: contacts, funnels, courses and automation rules
  • Which features sit on higher plans, such as automated webinars
  • Monthly versus annual billing, and what an annual discount means over a full year
  • Whether the price includes tax, and which currency you pay in
  • Whether Systeme.io adds any fee on top of your payment provider's own fees
  • Cancellation and refund terms
  • Whether you need extra sub-accounts or team access

Upgrade only when you hit a limit that blocks something you actually need.

Beginner setup path

  1. Pick one offer and one goal, such as collecting emails for a free guide or selling one course.
  2. Check the free plan limits on the official site.
  3. Create your account and choose a template for a single page.
  4. If you are selling, connect a payment method and check it works in your country. Help pages cover PayPal, Stripe and Razorpay.
  5. Set up your sending domain as the help pages describe, then write a short welcome email.
  6. Test the whole path as a customer: open the page, sign up or buy, and check the email and access arrive.
  7. Share it, then review your plan limits before adding more.
