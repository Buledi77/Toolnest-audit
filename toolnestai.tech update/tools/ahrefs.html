Ahrefs review: the backlink and keyword dataset, assessed

Ahrefs is the SEO tool people buy when links and content gaps decide the roadmap. Its backlink index and keyword database are the core product; everything else supports them. The trade-off is price, credit limits and a denser interface than Moz Pro.

Ahrefs overview

Ahrefs is a research platform built around two large datasets: the web index behind its backlink reports and the keyword database behind Keywords Explorer. Site Explorer, Content Explorer, Site Audit and Rank Tracker all draw on that foundation, which is why the product feels coherent rather than like a bundle of unrelated tools.

Why it stands out

If you need to know who links to a competitor, which pages earn links, or which keywords a rival ranks for that you do not, Ahrefs usually answers the question with fewer workarounds than a narrower SEO suite.

Who Ahrefs is best for, and who should skip it

Best for

  • Link building and link-gap analysis across competitors
  • Content strategy: finding topics, formats and pages that already earn search traffic
  • Keyword research where you need bulk filters, not just a difficulty score
  • Agencies that need credible organic-search data for pitches and audits
  • Site owners who want rank tracking and a technical audit in the same subscription

Skip it if

  • You mainly need your own rank tracking and crawl on the lowest budget; Moz Pro is cheaper
  • You need paid-search, local listings or broad content-production tooling; Semrush is broader
  • You need unlimited crawl depth or custom extraction; Screaming Frog is the specialist
  • You will only use a handful of keyword lookups a month; a free tool may be enough

Ahrefs pros and cons

Pros

  • The backlink index is the main reason to pay, and it is deep enough for real link-gap work
  • Keywords Explorer supports large sets, bulk filtering and export before you commit to a topic
  • Content Explorer surfaces pages and domains by traffic and links, which is useful for editorial planning
  • Site Audit and Rank Tracker mean you do not need a second subscription for routine monitoring
  • The interface, once learned, keeps related reports close together
  • Free tools and a free account make it possible to evaluate data coverage before paying

Cons

  • Pricing is high relative to a single-site workflow, and the cheapest Starter tier is deliberately limited
  • Credits are consumed by research, so broad exploration can exhaust a plan faster than expected
  • The interface assumes you know which report you need; beginners can flounder
  • Paid-search research is not the platform's strength
  • No permanent free tier with professional limits, and no self-hosted option
  • Some niche link or keyword coverage gaps are inevitable in any index

Features that matter in practice

Site Explorer

Organic keywords, top pages, backlinks and referring domains for any domain, with competitor comparison.

Keywords Explorer

Large keyword sets with volume, difficulty, traffic potential and SERP data, plus bulk filtering.

Site Audit

Scheduled crawls with issue tracking, JavaScript rendering and a health score.

Rank Tracker

Daily ranking monitoring for a defined keyword set, with plan-based limits.

Content Explorer

Search pages and domains by topic, traffic and links to find content opportunities.

Batch Analysis

Run the same report across many URLs or domains at once, useful for prospecting and audits.

Free plan and trial

Ahrefs offers a free account and a set of free tools with usage limits, but not a full free trial of the paid platform. The free account is useful for checking data coverage on a few domains; it is not enough for ongoing client work.

How it compares and what to try instead

Ahrefs is the specialist's research tool. It is usually the right buy when link or content data changes the decision, and the wrong buy when a simpler rank-tracking workflow would do.

Frequently asked questions

Is Ahrefs worth the price?

If link building or content-led SEO is a recurring, billable task, usually yes. If you only need rank tracking and a monthly crawl for one site, Moz Pro or a free crawler plus a keyword tool costs less.

How much does Ahrefs cost?

Entry prices run from $29 per month for Starter, $129 for Lite, $249 for Standard and $449 for Advanced, as displayed in November 2026. Annual billing changes the effective monthly rate, and plan limits vary.

Does Ahrefs have a free plan?

It offers a free account and free tools with limits, rather than a full trial of the paid platform. Check the current free-account limits on Ahrefs' site.

What is Ahrefs best at?

Backlink research and content gap analysis. Its index and filtering are the reason most teams choose it over a general SEO suite.
