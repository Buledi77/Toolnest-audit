Notion overview

Notion is a flexible workspace where notes, documents, task lists and simple databases live side by side. You can build a client portal, a content calendar or a personal planner from the same building blocks.

Why we recommend it

It replaces three or four scattered apps with one workspace you can shape yourself, which suits solo workers the most.

Who Notion is best for, and who should skip it

Best for

  • Freelancers and small teams who want notes, tasks and client pages together
  • People who like starting from a template and adjusting it
  • Anyone replacing scattered documents and spreadsheets

Not best for

  • Large teams that need strict project tracking with timelines and workload views
  • People who want a tool that works perfectly with no setup
  • Anyone who needs heavy offline use

Notion pros and cons

Pros

  • One home for notes and to-dos
  • Templates to start fast
  • Good for solo work

Cons

  • Can feel like a lot to set up at first
  • Free and paid limits change, so check current plans
  • Project tracking is less strict than in dedicated project tools
