Head-to-head comparison

Ahrefs vs Semrush

Ahrefs is the sharper research instrument for links and content. Semrush is the broader marketing platform. If your work is organic search, Ahrefs is usually enough; if you also handle paid search, local or multi-channel reporting, Semrush earns its higher entry price.

The short answer

Our verdict

Choose Ahrefs when the question is 'who links to them and what do they rank for?' Choose Semrush when the question is broader: 'what is this competitor doing across search, ads and content, and how do I report it to a client?' Both are credible; the wrong choice is paying for modules you never open.

Choose Ahrefs if…

Link research, content gaps and organic keyword analysis are the recurring tasks, and you want the cleanest path from data to a content or link plan.

  • Backlinks and content-led SEO are the core of your work
  • You prefer focused, data-dense reports over broad menus
  • You need large keyword sets with strong filtering
  • You do not need PPC or local marketing modules

Choose Semrush if…

You need one platform for SEO, PPC research, content briefs, local listings and client reporting, even if each individual dataset is not the category leader.

  • You need organic, paid and content research in one subscription
  • You manage multiple clients and produce regular reports
  • You want local SEO and AI-search visibility features
  • You value breadth over a single best-in-class dataset

Which workflow fits you

Ahrefs has a clear centre of gravity: the backlink index and the keyword database. Most of its workflows radiate from those two assets. That focus is a strength when your job is organic growth, because the reports are built around the comparisons that matter: your site versus competitors, your keywords versus theirs, your content versus the pages already ranking.

Semrush's centre of gravity is the marketing team's whole week. A single project can hold organic rankings, paid-search ads, backlinks, site audits, content briefs and local listings. The cost of that breadth is complexity and, at the entry tier, a higher price than Ahrefs Lite. It is also why Semrush is common in agencies: one platform can serve several service lines and produce a client-ready report.

Data quality arguments are usually overdone. Both vendors maintain large indexes and both have gaps. For most sites, the practical test is coverage in your specific market: check ten of your competitor domains in each tool during a trial and see which one surfaces the links and keywords you can verify. That beats any generic benchmark.

Budget matters too. Ahrefs' Starter tier is inexpensive but deliberately limited; for professional use, compare Lite and above. Semrush's entry SEO plan is capable but its higher tiers unlock the limits that heavy users hit. In both cases, the headline price is less informative than the limit you will reach first.

Frequently asked questions

Is Ahrefs or Semrush better for SEO?

Ahrefs is usually preferred for backlink and content research; Semrush is preferred for breadth, paid-search research and client reporting. Neither is universally better.

Which is cheaper?

Ahrefs Lite starts lower than Semrush's SEO plan, but Ahrefs' cheapest Starter tier is limited. Compare the tier that covers your real usage, not the entry line.

Does Semrush include rank tracking?

Yes, Position Tracking is part of the platform with plan-based limits on tracked keywords and frequency. Ahrefs includes Rank Tracker with its own limits.

Can I use both?

Larger teams sometimes do, but it is rarely necessary at the start. Pick the one that matches your primary work, then add the other only when a specific gap is costing you money.
