Moz Pro vs Semrush

Moz Pro is a focused SEO workspace for the site you own. Semrush is a wider marketing-intelligence suite for competitive research. The right choice depends on whether you need to monitor your own search performance or investigate a market.

The short answer

Our verdict

For a single site owner who tracks rankings, crawls their own pages and researches keywords, Moz Pro covers the recurring work at a lower monthly price. For an agency or marketer who must explain competitor traffic, paid search or content gaps, Semrush replaces several tools and justifies the higher subscription.

Neither is automatically better. The deciding question is whether the extra datasets match work you are paid to do.

Choose Moz Pro if…

Your recurring job is your own site: which keywords moved, which pages broke and what to fix next.

  • You track rankings, crawl your own site and research keywords in one place
  • You want a complete SEO workspace at the lowest monthly price in this comparison
  • You prefer fewer modules and a shorter learning curve
  • You do not need paid-search or competitor-advertising research

Choose Semrush if…

You need to explain a competitor's traffic, ad spend or content gaps, or you handle several clients in one subscription.

  • You need competitor, keyword, backlink and paid-search research together
  • You manage several client sites and need consolidated reporting
  • You produce content at volume and want briefs beside the keyword data
  • You will actually use the wider dataset, not just admire it

Who should skip each product — and who should choose neither

Skip Moz Pro if…

  • You need to explain competitors' organic traffic, ad spend or content gaps
  • You need the largest available backlink index for link-gap work
  • You produce content briefs at volume and want templates beside the data
  • You manage many clients and need consolidated, branded reporting

Skip Semrush if…

  • Your SEO is one site on a tight budget; the extra modules go unused
  • You only need a technical crawl; a dedicated crawler can be cheaper
  • Your team will not complete onboarding to the wider interface
  • You need the simplest possible interface from day one

Choose neither when

Choose neither if your only recurring job is a technical crawl. A dedicated crawler such as Screaming Frog can go deeper for a flat annual licence and may be cheaper than either subscription. If you only need backlink analysis, compare Ahrefs first. If you are not doing recurring SEO, a free account or a specialist consultant may be enough.

How to decide: buyer criteria that matter

Ignore the feature list on the pricing page until you have answered these questions. They decide which plan you actually need.

The job you repeat

Monitoring your own site is a different purchase from investigating a market. Buy for the task that fills your week.

Coverage in your market

Test sample keywords and domains from your own niche. A large global index does not guarantee good coverage where you work.

Projects and people

Count client sites, campaigns and seats. A plan that fits one site can be useless for an agency.

The limits you will hit

Tracked keywords, crawl pages, export rows and report refreshes are capped by tier. Compare the limit you will actually reach.

Reporting and exports

If clients see the output, check white-label options, scheduled reports and CSV or API access before paying.

Billing term

Annual billing lowers the monthly equivalent but commits your cash. Check the renewal price and cancellation terms.

Genuine limitations

Every tool is a compromise. These are the drawbacks we would want to know before paying.

Moz Pro limitations

  • The datasets are narrower than Semrush's, especially paid search and content
  • Backlink coverage can differ from larger indexes in some niches
  • Fewer integrations and export options than a large suite
  • Plan limits on campaigns, tracked keywords and crawl pages still apply

Semrush limitations

  • The subscription costs more at every comparable tier
  • The interface is broad and assumes you know which report you want
  • Plan caps on projects, keywords and reports can bite at scale
  • You may pay for modules your workflow never touches

Switching, export and migration

Neither vendor offers a one-click migration of campaign history. The data that survives is what you export: keywords, URLs, crawl issues and report files. Budget a few hours of setup for one site and considerably more for an agency portfolio.

Moving from Semrush to Moz Pro

  • Export keyword lists, ranking history and crawl issues as CSV before you cancel.
  • Rebuild campaigns in Moz Pro; campaign history does not transfer between vendors.
  • Expect backlink counts and keyword volumes to differ because the indexes are different.
  • Run both accounts for one billing cycle so reporting is not interrupted.

Moving from Moz Pro to Semrush

  • Export saved keywords, reports and any client-facing PDFs before you cancel.
  • Recreate projects and keyword lists in Semrush; historical rank data does not import.
  • Check the contract term and cancellation window so you do not pay for an unused renewal.
  • Tell clients if the reporting format changes, and rebrand templates before the first report.
Plan for overlap. Keep the old subscription active until the new account has produced at least one full reporting cycle. The overlap costs money, but it protects your history and your client deliverables.

Use-case conclusion

Single site, tight budget

Moz Pro — Standard covers rank tracking, crawl and keyword research at the lowest full-workspace price here.

Agency with several clients

Semrush — multi-project reporting and competitor datasets justify the higher subscription.

Competitor traffic and ad research

Semrush — paid-search and organic competitor views are native to the platform.

Simple monthly SEO report

Moz Pro — campaign dashboards are faster to explain to a non-specialist client.

Technical audit of one site

Neither, necessarily — a dedicated crawler such as Screaming Frog may be cheaper and deeper.

Backlink-led link building

Semrush or Ahrefs — compare link indexes before choosing; Moz is the narrower option here.

Alternatives worth considering

If neither product matches the job, start with the workflow rather than the brand. A crawler, a link index and a full research suite solve different problems.

Frequently asked questions

Is Moz Pro cheaper than Semrush?

Yes at every comparable self-serve tier. Moz Pro Standard is $99 per month, or $79 per month billed yearly, against Semrush SEO at $139 per month, or $117.33 billed yearly. Semrush includes more datasets, so the lower Moz price buys a narrower toolset, not the same product for less.

Does Semrush have a free plan?

Semrush offers a limited free account with daily caps on queries and reports, plus a 7-day trial on paid plans. Moz Pro advertises a free trial rather than a permanent free tier. Free access on either is for evaluation, not ongoing client work.

Can I replace Semrush with Moz Pro?

Only if you do not use Semrush's paid-search, content or competitor-research modules. For rank tracking, site crawl and keyword research on your own domain, Moz Pro is sufficient. Teams that bill competitor research usually need the wider dataset.

Which is better for a beginner?

Moz Pro is easier to learn because there are fewer modules and the campaign structure is obvious. Semrush has strong documentation and free courses, but the interface assumes you already know which report you want.

Can I migrate my history between them?

Not automatically. Export keyword lists, crawl issues and report files before cancelling, then rebuild campaigns in the new tool. Historical rankings and backlink counts will not match exactly because each vendor uses a different index.
