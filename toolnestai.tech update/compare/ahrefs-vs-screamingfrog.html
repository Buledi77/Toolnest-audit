Ahrefs vs Screaming Frog

Ahrefs answers 'what should we target?' Screaming Frog answers 'what is broken on the site?' The overlap is site auditing, and even there the desktop crawler goes deeper for less money.

Jump to pricingJump to verdict
Last reviewed November 2026How we research

The short answer

Our verdict

If you can only buy one and your site is technically messy, buy Screaming Frog first: it is cheaper, it finds the problems blocking indexation, and the free tier may be enough. If your site is technically sound but you do not know which keywords or links to pursue, Ahrefs is the better spend.

Choose Ahrefs if…

The bottleneck is opportunity: which keywords, which competitors, which links. You need a research database and ongoing rank tracking.

  • You need backlink, keyword and competitor research
  • You want a cloud suite with rank tracking and content data
  • You manage multiple sites and need shareable reports
  • You will use the data to plan work, not only audit it
Visit Ahrefs (opens the official site in a new tab)

Choose Screaming Frog if…

The bottleneck is implementation: crawl budget, redirects, canonicals, rendering, orphan pages and migration QA on a site you already understand.

  • You need a technical crawl with full export control
  • You are auditing a migration, JavaScript site or huge URL set
  • You want a low annual cost rather than a subscription
  • You already have keyword and link data elsewhere

Which workflow fits you

The buying logic is unusual because the cheaper tool is not the lesser tool. Screaming Frog at £199 per year is a specialised instrument that outperforms Ahrefs' audit on crawl control, custom extraction and staging comparisons. Ahrefs at $129 or more per month is a data subscription whose value comes from what it knows about the wider web, not from how deeply it can crawl one site.

For a new site with clean architecture, the first constraint is usually demand, not technical debt. Ahrefs tells you which pages to build. For an older site with thousands of parameter URLs, the first constraint is crawl efficiency. Screaming Frog tells you which URLs to fix, merge or block. Spending on the wrong side is common: a technically clean site owner paying for crawl depth they do not need, or a messy enterprise site relying on a cloud audit with page quotas.

There is a middle path. Screaming Frog's free tier handles small audits at no cost, and Ahrefs Free gives a limited look at links and keywords. A new site can use both free tiers for a month, then buy the one whose limits it hits first. That is a more honest test than comparing feature lists.

If you already own one, the upgrade case for the other is narrow. Ahrefs users should buy Screaming Frog when a migration or JavaScript rendering problem exceeds the cloud audit. Screaming Frog users should buy Ahrefs when keyword and link research becomes a recurring billable task rather than an occasional lookup.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need Ahrefs if I have Screaming Frog?

Yes, if you need keyword or backlink research. Screaming Frog audits pages; it does not tell you what the market is searching for or who links to competitors.

Do I need Screaming Frog if I have Ahrefs?

Not always. Ahrefs Site Audit covers routine checks. A dedicated crawler becomes worthwhile for large sites, JavaScript rendering, custom extraction and migration comparisons.

Which is cheaper?

Screaming Frog is far cheaper in year one: £199 per year versus a monthly Ahrefs subscription. It also does a narrower job, so the comparison is only meaningful against the task you need done.

Can Screaming Frog pull Ahrefs data?

Yes. Screaming Frog can integrate with external link-metric APIs, including Ahrefs, to enrich crawl data. That is not a substitute for an Ahrefs subscription and its research interface.
