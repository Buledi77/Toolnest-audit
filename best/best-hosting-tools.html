Best hosting for a first website: what to check before you buy

A low first invoice is easy to advertise. The useful comparison is the renewal rate, the term you must commit to and the resources your site will actually use.

The promo price is not the price you will pay

Hosting is a long-term purchase sold with a short-term price. The number on the banner often requires a multi-year upfront payment, and the renewal rate can be several times higher. That does not make the offer bad; it makes the headline incomplete.

We publish a full review for one hosting provider, Hostinger. We would rather show one reviewed option and explain the trade-offs than pad a shortlist with providers we have not checked. The buying criteria below apply to any host you compare.

Note. A low first-term price can be real value, but it is only comparable once you add the renewal rate, the term length and the features you actually need. This page separates those numbers instead of quoting the headline only.

What matters when choosing a first hosting plan

Renewal price, not just the promo

Ask what the plan costs after the introductory term. Multiply the renewal rate by the years you expect to stay, then compare the total.

Term length and upfront cash

The lowest monthly price usually requires the longest commitment. Check the refund window and whether you are comfortable paying for years in advance.

SSL, domain and email

A free SSL certificate is table stakes. Confirm whether a domain and business email are included, and what they cost at renewal.

Resources you will actually use

Storage, bandwidth, websites, databases and email accounts matter more than a long feature list. A single site and a small store have different needs.

Backups, staging and migration

Check whether backups are automatic, whether staging is included, and whether the host will migrate an existing site for free.

Support and control panel

A beginner-friendly panel reduces friction; a developer may prefer SSH and raw server access. Check support hours and the channel you would actually use.

When Hostinger is not the right fit

If you need managed WordPress with a host that handles updates, caching and security for you, compare specialist managed WordPress plans rather than the cheapest shared tier. The support and maintenance model is different, and so is the price.

If you expect sustained high traffic or need root access, a VPS or cloud plan is a better fit than shared hosting. Hostinger publishes VPS options, but the plan you choose should follow the workload, not the other way round.

If you mainly want a drag-and-drop site and never want to see a control panel, a website builder may be the simpler purchase. Hosting is the infrastructure; the builder is the editing experience.

If you need a specific compliance, data-residency or uptime commitment, check the contract and ask the provider directly. A generic buying guide cannot verify those terms for you.

The bottom line

For a first site, choose the plan whose renewal rate you can still justify in year two, not the one with the lowest first invoice. Hostinger's Premium tier is a sensible starting point if you accept the term; move up only when a real site or store needs the resources.

Frequently asked questions

Is Hostinger good for a first website?

It is a reasonable fit for a first site if you want hosting, a domain and a builder in one account at a low entry price. The important caveats are the long promotional term and the higher renewal rate. Check the checkout total, not just the monthly banner.

Why is there only one host on this page?

Because we have only published a full review for one provider. Adding names we have not checked would make the shortlist look bigger without making it more useful. The criteria apply to any host you compare.

Is the promotional price the price I will pay?

Only for the initial term, and usually only if you pay for a long period upfront. The renewal rate is higher. Add the renewal cost and any domain or email charges before deciding.

Do I need cloud hosting instead of shared hosting?

If your site has outgrown shared resources, needs root access or expects sustained traffic, yes. For a first blog or small business site, a shared plan is usually enough to start.

Does Hostinger include a free domain and SSL?

Hostinger's plans list SSL, and domain registration is available in the same account. The exact inclusion depends on the plan and term, so confirm it at checkout.

Did you test Hostinger's speed and uptime?

No. We did not run an independent performance test. This guide compares published plan features and prices; it does not claim a measured uptime or speed result.
