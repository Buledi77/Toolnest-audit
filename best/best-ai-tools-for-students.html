Best Tools for Students

Students need help with learning, organising and writing, not shortcuts that break school rules. These tools cover writing support, notes and deadlines.

AM

How we picked

These picks come from the tools ToolNest currently covers, chosen on official product information. We favour tools that support learning and organisation over ones that only produce finished text.

  • Helps you learn, not just finish.
  • Easy to start without training.
  • Works with the way students already work.
  • Honest about where it falls short.

Our picks

1

Grammarly

Writing

Catches spelling, grammar and clarity problems in essays and emails.

Best for
Anyone writing in English for school
Skip it if
You want long in-depth style reports on a thesis.
Why it ranks here
Writing is a task nearly every student does each week, and it works inside the apps they already use.
Better option in this list
Notion, if your bigger problem is keeping notes and deadlines in order.

2

Notion

Productivity

Keeps notes, deadlines and study plans in one place.

Best for
Students organising several classes
Skip it if
You want a very simple notes app with no setup.
Why it ranks here
It organises classes, notes and deadlines in one place, though it takes some setup.
Better option in this list
Grammarly, if writing quality matters more than organisation. Descript, if you mostly record lectures.
Try Notion (opens the official site in a new tab)Read the Notion review →
3

Descript

Voice & Video

Turns a recorded lecture, interview or study session into a transcript you can search and edit.

Best for
Students who record lectures, interviews or group projects
Skip it if
You never record audio or video.
Why it ranks here
It only helps if you record audio or video, but for lectures and interviews that is its strongest use.
Better option in this list
Grammarly or Notion, if you do not record anything.
